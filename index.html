Introduction · How to read these results

Continuum builds models that forecast where a person's health is going: mortality risk, daily function, physical strength, and cognition, all from the history they already have. Before any of that reaches a product, it has to prove itself on data we cannot touch: public longitudinal aging studies that have followed hundreds of thousands of people for decades, across studies spanning Europe, the Americas, and Asia, with real outcomes on record.

Every result on this page follows the same rules. Models are scored on held-out people they never trained on. Predictions are made from earlier study waves only and checked against what actually happened at later ones. And where a number needs a caveat, the caveat sits next to the number.

Five groups of results so far: mortality prediction in Mexico, China, and Costa Rica; daily-function forecasting in the United States; next-wave IADL-difficulty prediction in Mexico; next-wave grip-strength and cognition forecasts in Europe, South Korea, and India; and a fasting-glucose pilot in the United States.

HRS validation · Future Self + Case Matcher

Forecasting daily function: ADL & IADL

Validated on the Health and Retirement Study, a US survey re-interviewing 40,000+ adults every two years since 1992. Two stages: Future Self forecasts where a marker goes next from the person's own 3-point history; Case Matcher grounds that forecast against the 50 most similar training people's realized trajectories. Person-level holdout: 31,005 training people, 7,752 test people. A test person's own record is never in the training pool, so no one can be their own neighbor.

ADL / IADLCounts of activities of daily living (basic self-care) and instrumental activities (money, medications, meals) a person has difficulty with. The standard HRS functional measures.
Trend baselineThe comparison that matters: extend the person's own trend line and call that the forecast. Our forecast beats it on both markers.
Direction of changeOf people who actually changed, how often the forecast pointed the right way, better or worse.
80% coverageHow often the stated 80% uncertainty range contained the true value. ADL/IADL run ~0.90, slightly conservative.

Grounding turns a direction-blind forecast into one that carries drift. ADL and IADL counts move slowly, and most people report no change between waves. A forecast tuned to minimize average error therefore predicts roughly no change for nearly everyone. Directional accuracy is scored only among people who did change, so a flat prediction is counted wrong every time. That is why the self-forecast sits at 0.171 for ADL and 0.096 for IADL rather than near chance. The 50 nearest real trajectories are not flat: real lives drift, and averaging over them gives the forecast a direction it would never produce alone. That lifts directional accuracy to 0.774 and 0.779, and flips R² positive, at the cost of slightly higher average error.

MHAS · Next-wave IADL difficulty

Forecasting next-wave IADL difficulty

Across 20 household-held-out splits in MHAS, a model using demographic and longitudinal health information from Waves 1–5 achieved a mean AUROC of 0.852 for predicting any IADL difficulty at Wave 6. The full model outperformed the baseline in all 20 splits.

What this measures IADL difficulty Harmonized four-item IADL measure

The outcome is whether a respondent reported any difficulty at Wave 6 on the harmonized four-item IADL measure: managing money, taking medication, shopping for groceries, or making hot meals. Respondents had to be alive and interviewed at both Waves 5 and 6.

The analysis did not require participants to be difficulty-free at Wave 5, so it predicts later IADL status, not strictly new-onset loss of independence. The full model adds demographics, cognition, chronic conditions, ADL measures, and other health variables, so the gap over the baseline does not isolate the contribution of history alone.

The split is by household, roughly 80/20, so members of the same household never appear in both training and testing. The full model used 77 features from Waves 1–5 (demographics, education, prior IADL and ADL, self-rated health, depression, cognition, chronic conditions); no Wave 6 information was used as input. Median follow-up from Wave 5 to Wave 6 was 37 months (range 33–39). On the primary split the full model scored 0.860 versus 0.693 for the baseline; across the 20 splits it ranged 0.841–0.864.

Trained and evaluated entirely within MHAS; a separate analysis from the mortality study. Note that 0.852 is AUROC, not 85.2% accuracy: it means the model ranked a randomly selected future-difficulty case above a non-case about 85.2% of the time.

SHARE · KLoSA · LASI-DAD · Next-wave forecasts

Forecasting future cognition and physical function

Across independent aging cohorts, longitudinal health models explained 77% of future grip-strength variation in SHARE, 71.5% in KLoSA, and 74% of future cognitive variation in LASI-DAD. In each cohort the model won all 20 repeated held-out splits.

Grip strength is a standard marker of physical function, frailty, and functional aging. Amber ticks below mark each persistence baseline: the score from assuming the person's latest measurement simply carries forward.

CALERIE · Early biomarker pilot

Forecasting fasting glucose

Using baseline clinical information, the model predicted directly measured fasting glucose 12 months later in 199 CALERIE participants. Every prediction was out-of-fold, meaning each participant was evaluated by a model that had not trained on them.

Predictions differed from the true Month 12 glucose measurement by 3.11 mg/dL on average. The model reduced average error by 15.8% compared with carrying the baseline measurement forward, and a calibration slope of 0.964 is close to the ideal value of 1.00.

What was used as inputBaseline fasting glucose, HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, age, sex, and known randomized study assignment.
What was predictedThe participant's directly measured fasting glucose after 12 months, reported in mg/dL.
What was the baselineAssume the participant's future glucose stays equal to their baseline glucose measurement.
Important limitationA small research pilot in a selected group of generally healthy participants. Observed Month 12 glucose ranged from 67 to 97 mg/dL. The result does not establish performance for people with diabetes, clinical utility, or individualized treatment-response prediction.

About these numbers. All metrics are computed on held-out data. MHAS, CHARLS, and H-CRELES are publicly documented longitudinal studies of aging; mortality results shown are from the corrected landmark analysis, with each model trained and evaluated within its own cohort (no cross-country external validation is claimed). HRS results use Harmonized HRS (Version D, 1992–2021) and the RAND HRS Longitudinal File; functional markers only; the ADL/IADL work makes no disease or mortality claims. Standardization and neighbor search use training statistics only; fixed seed; the pipeline reproduces every number. This is a working research page; sections will be added as they are finalized.
